Field lines through a surface — the building block for Gauss's Law.

Master Electric Flux in 18 Minutes
Visualizing Φ = E · A · cos(θ)

Electric flux (Φ) quantifies the total number of electric field lines passing through a given surface. It captures both the field's strength and the surface's orientation relative to it — a surface tilted away from the field "catches" fewer lines, exactly like rainfall through a tilted window.

Why direction matters
Flux is a scalar, but depends on the angle θ between the field E and the surface's area vector A — maximum when parallel (θ=0°), zero when perpendicular (θ=90°).
Sign convention
Flux is positive when field lines exit a closed surface, negative when they enter — this sign convention is essential for Gauss's Law.
Units
Measured in N·m²/C (or V·m) in SI units — always check units match when solving PYQs with mixed given quantities.
Common JEE/NEET trap
Students often use the angle to the surface plane instead of the angle to the normal (area vector) — this is the single most repeated mistake across 42 PYQs.
📍 Analogy: rainfall through a window✓ Takeaway: only enclosed charge matters
Φ = E · A · cos θ
General formula — E: field intensity (N/C), A: area (m²), θ: angle between E and the normal.
Φ = Q_enc / ε₀
Gauss's Law — total flux through any closed surface depends only on the enclosed charge, never on shape or size.
Shortcut: for a cube with a charge at its center, flux per face = Q/(6ε₀). For a charge at a corner, flux through the cube = Q/(24ε₀).
